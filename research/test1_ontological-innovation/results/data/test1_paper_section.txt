Test 1 Results: Ontological Innovation in Sensory Modalities

The collection produced 168 raw response records from seven models. After completion and length checks and normalization by proposal name, the analysis set contained 79 unique proposals. Evaluation combined structural decomposition, embedding-space analysis, cosine similarity, and literature traceability.

Structural decomposition: 48/79 proposals (60.8%) matched at least one recombination pattern. The overlapping indicators comprised 24 range extensions, 45 hybrids, and 14 functional recombinations.

Embedding-space analysis: all 79/79 proposals (100.0%) lay inside the convex hull of the eight training-modality embeddings in the three-component PCA space. Mean nearest-modality cosine similarity was 0.366 (SD=0.079).

Literature traceability: at the calibrated threshold of 0.50, 15/79 proposals (19.0%) retrieved at least one scholarly-corpus match. All 15 matched proposals had a strongest match from the strong-evidence corpus tier; the mean best-match score was 0.550.

Conclusion: no proposal met the notebook's strict novelty classification. The result is driven principally by universal hull membership, supplemented by structural recombination markers and literature matches where retrieval crossed the calibrated threshold. This supports a constrained-manifold interpretation for this prompt, ontology, model set, and analysis protocol; it does not establish universal limits on model creativity.
